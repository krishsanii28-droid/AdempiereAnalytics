## Model comparison on this CPU

26 synthetic weekly series of 156 weeks, forecasting 4 weeks ahead, 6 successive hold-out windows per series (156 windows). Reproduce with `python -m insightai.forecast.benchmark <model ids>`.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Naive (baseline) | 4.135 | 1.171 | | |
| Moving average, 8 weeks (baseline) | 4.075 | 0.964 | | |
| Seasonal naive, 52 weeks (baseline) | 4.250 | 1.044 | | |
| `amazon/chronos-bolt-tiny` | 3.594 | 0.834 | 0.16 s | 79% |
| `amazon/chronos-bolt-small` | 3.547 | 0.829 | 0.56 s | 81% |

| `amazon/chronos-2` | 3.443 | 0.818 | 3.8 s | 76% |

MASE scales each error by the in-sample error of repeating last week's value, so below 1 is better than that.

**Choice: `amazon/chronos-bolt-small`.** It is about 110 times faster than `chronos-t5-small` here with slightly better MASE. `chronos-2` is slightly more accurate (0.818 against 0.829) at about 7 times the inference time and a larger download; switch with `model` in `config.toml` if that trade is wanted.

**What this does and does not show.** These numbers are measured on *synthetic* demand. They show the pipeline works and that the model beats simple baselines on series with the patterns the generator produces. They say nothing about accuracy on real GardenWorld demand, which cannot be measured (see below). The model beat the strongest baseline in 97 of 156 windows, so on a single product in a single month the baseline is often as good.

## Real data: nothing can be forecast yet

A series is forecast only if it spans at least 26 weeks and has demand in at least 8 of them. Of the 33 product and warehouse combinations with stock or shipments:

- 27 have no completed customer shipments at all.
- 6 have shipments in only 1 or 2 weeks (5 in a single week).

(Before Stage 7 these counts read 37 and 10: service and expense items on shipment documents were being counted as stock demand. The demand view now holds stocked items only.)

So in real mode every row reads "Not forecast" with its reason, no accuracy is reported, and the dashboard's "Products at stockout risk" card shows "Data unavailable". The first version of the rule only checked the span of the series; it let through four 2018-2019 products whose history was one shipment followed by seven years of zeros, and "evaluated" them at near-zero error. The demand-weeks rule was added to stop that.

## Synthetic mode

- Generated by `insightai/forecast/synthetic.py`: one series per product and warehouse that has stock, with a profile chosen by a seeded generator (steady, seasonal, rising, falling, intermittent) and an occasional bulk-order spike.
- The demand level is derived from current stock so the demo shows a spread of situations. Stock on hand and open purchase quantities are the real ADempiere figures.
- Nothing is written to the database. The series are written to `data/synthetic/demand_weekly_<client>.csv` with a `SYNTHETIC` marker on every row.
- Real and synthetic results are separate files (`data/forecast_<client>_real.json`, `..._synthetic.json`). The API returns `synthetic: true`, the page shows a banner above everything derived from it, and the chart title says "SYNTHETIC DEMAND".
- The KPI card among the real figures always comes from the real file.

Current synthetic result for GardenWorld: 26 series, 7 high risk, 6 medium, 13 low, 6 flagged as excess stock.

## How a forecast is built

1. Daily demand from `insightai.v_demand_daily`: completed customer shipments (`C-`) minus customer returns (`C+`) of stocked items, by `MovementDate`. Voided and reversed documents are already excluded by the view.
2. Summed into weeks (Monday to Sunday), from the first week with demand to the last *complete* week. Missing weeks are zero. A week where returns exceed shipments is set to zero and counted.
3. Outlier weeks are flagged (median absolute deviation rule, threshold 3.5) and shown on the chart. They are not removed.
4. The model forecasts 4 weeks at the 10th, 50th and 90th percentiles.
5. Available stock is `QtyOnHand − QtyReserved` from `M_Storage`.
6. Incoming stock is open purchase order line quantity promised between 30 days ago and the end of the horizon. Older or undated open lines are reported but not counted (GardenWorld has 381 units on 2019 purchase orders).
7. Risk: **High** if stock plus incoming is below the median forecast demand, **Medium** if below the 90th percentile, otherwise **Low**. The probability shown is read off the forecast percentiles and reported as "over 90%" or "under 10%" outside them.
8. Stock lasting more than 26 weeks at the median forecast rate is flagged as excess.

All thresholds are in `config.toml` under `[forecast]`.

## Limits

- The horizon total for each percentile is the sum of the weekly percentiles. That overstates the width of the range, most visibly for intermittent products.
- Incoming stock is treated as arriving at the start of the horizon.
- No lead times, safety stock, promotions or prices are used. This is a screening list, not a replenishment plan.
- Forecasts are per product and warehouse; there is no pooling across warehouses.
- Weekly only. Daily data would be too sparse.

## Fallback

If PyTorch or Chronos is not installed, or the model is neither in `models/` nor downloadable, the job uses the statistical baseline (seasonal naive with two years of history, otherwise moving average, otherwise naive) with a range taken from that baseline's own past errors. The result file records `model.used = "baseline"` and the reason, and the page shows it.

Once a model has been downloaded it is loaded from `models/` without contacting Hugging Face, so the demo works offline.

## Running it

```
powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File scripts\setup_service.ps1 -ML
.venv\Scripts\python.exe -m insightai.forecast
```

Or use **Run forecast** on the dashboard. Results are a snapshot: stock and purchase quantities are as of the run, so re-run after stock changes.
